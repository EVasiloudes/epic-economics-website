## 🎨 Visual Direction Summary

The visual identity marries **Soviet constructivist poster design** with **contemporary protest graphics** and **editorial print layouts**. Think: bold geometric blocks of red and black on warm paper stock, oversized typography that functions as graphic element, and the tactile imperfection of letterpress and screen printing.

The overarching metaphor is **the manifesto as visual language** — text as weapon, print as power, paper as platform. The design should feel like something you'd find stapled to a telephone pole outside an economics faculty, not something designed in a Figma file. Intentional rawness over pixel perfection.

## 🏛 Primary Aesthetic Territories

### 1. Soviet Constructivist Poster Art (1920s–1930s)
The OG protest graphic design movement. Characteristics: bold red/black/cream palettes, diagonal compositions, photography + typography integration, revolutionary urgency.

- **Key artists:** Alexander Rodchenko, El Lissitzky, the Stenberg brothers
- **What to steal:** The colour palette (red, black, cream), the typographic boldness, the diagonal energy, the fusion of image and text into a single graphic statement
- **What to leave:** The specific Soviet iconography (hammers, sickles, heroic workers) — we're referencing the aesthetic language, not the politics
- **Search terms:** "soviet constructivist posters," "rodchenko graphic design," "russian avant garde typography"

### 2. Contemporary Protest Graphics (2010s–present)
What does dissent look like today? DIY screen-printed placards, Extinction Rebellion's bold graphic identity, Black Lives Matter protest signage, climate strike visuals.

- **Key references:** Extinction Rebellion's design system (bold type, limited palette, high contrast), the Women's March pink pussyhat + bold typography, Occupy Wall Street's handmade aesthetic
- **What to steal:** The urgency, the DIY energy, the use of single bold colour + black, the sense that these graphics were made quickly with purpose
- **What to leave:** Specific political movements' iconography — the show critiques economics broadly, not any single movement
- **Search terms:** "extinction rebellion graphic design," "contemporary protest placards," "activism graphic design 2020s"

### 3. Contemporary Editorial / Print Design
High-end magazine and book design that treats typography as the primary visual element — oversized headlines, generous margins, considered paper stock.

- **Key references:** The New York Times Magazine redesigns, Apartamento magazine, Eye Magazine, anything printed by Park Books or Lars Müller Publishers
- **What to steal:** Typographic hierarchy as the main design tool, generous whitespace, the confidence to let a single word fill a page
- **What to leave:** The preciousness — this is Fringe, not a coffee table book
- **Search terms:** "editorial typography design," "bold magazine layout," "typography-first web design"

### 4. Theatre & Fringe Poster Design
What actually works on the Royal Mile? Study the posters that stop you mid-stride — almost always bold typography + one striking image + high contrast + clear hierarchy.

- **Key references:** Edinburgh Fringe brochure covers, National Theatre poster campaigns, Complicité and Punchdrunk visual identities
- **What to steal:** The discipline — one image, one typeface, three colours max. The Fringe punishes visual complexity.
- **What to leave:** The "comedy show" visual language (yellow/black, wacky fonts, headshots) — this is theatre, not stand-up
- **Search terms:** "edinburgh fringe poster design," "theatre poster typography," "contemporary theatre branding"

## 🔗 Reference URLs (Actionable Sources for the UI Designer)

1. **Extinction Rebellion Design System**
   https://extinctionrebellion.uk/design/
   — Open-source activist design system. Study their use of a single bold colour, condensed typography, and high-contrast layouts. Their "design for non-designers" philosophy aligns with the show's accessible rigour.

2. **Soviet Posters Collection — The Art Institute of Chicago**
   https://www.artic.edu/collection?q=soviet+poster
   — Museum-quality scans of constructivist posters. Filter by Rodchenko for the strongest typographic references.

3. **Avenir Next Specimen — Linotype**
   https://www.linotype.com/1308886/avenir-next-family.html
   — Understand the weight range available. The heavy weights (800, 900) are critical for the poster/placard aesthetic.

4. **Crimson Pro on Google Fonts**
   https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Crimson+Pro
   — Preview the variable font. Notice the sharp serifs, generous x-height, and how weight 500 strikes the best balance for screen body text.

5. **"Design for the Real World" — Protest Graphics Gallery (search reference)**
   Search: "contemporary protest graphic design 2024 2025"
   — Pinterest board or Are.na channel of current protest aesthetics. Focus on the handmade, urgent, high-contrast examples.

## 🎭 Mood Keywords

Hand these to any designer and they'll know exactly where to aim:

- **Manifesto energy**
- **Bold typographic contrast**
- **High-contrast colour blocking**
- **Letterpress / screen print texture**
- **Revolutionary calm** (urgency without chaos)
- **Printed matter, not digital product**
- **Red as argument, not decoration**
- **Space as confidence** (generous margins = intellectual confidence)
- **Imperfect alignment** (slightly off-register, like a hurried print run)
- **Paper, ink, urgency**

## 📸 Imagery Direction (for the UI Designer)

If photography is used on the site (hero images, press page, cast photos):

- **Treatment:** High-contrast black and white or duotone (red + black). No full-colour photography — it dilutes the palette.
- **Style:** Candid, backstage, in-performance, not posed press shots. Grain is good. Motion blur is good. The energy of live theatre, not the polish of a headshot.
- **Integration:** Images should sit on the Parchment background with generous whitespace around them. No image fills the full viewport width — let the typography own the edges.
- **Framing:** Asymmetric, off-centre compositions. Rodchenko-style diagonal angles where appropriate.

## 🚫 Visual Anti-References (What NOT to Reference)

- ❌ Stripe / Linear / Vercel-style SaaS landing pages (gradients, glass, soft shadows)
- ❌ Apple-style product pages (pure white, ultra-minimal, centered everything)
- ❌ Fintech / banking visual language (blue, trust signals, smiling stock photos)
- ❌ Bootstrap / Tailwind UI defaults (generic, recognizable, unopinionated)
- ❌ Dribbble "trending" design (the current aesthetic monoculture)
- ❌ Anything with purple-to-blue gradients or frosted glass
